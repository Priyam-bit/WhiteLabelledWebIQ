The DASH Diet: What to Eat

A practical guide to Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension — what the evidence actually says, and how to build it into ordinary meals.

1,284 words · 6 min read

The DASH eating plan — Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension — has been recommended by the NIH for more than two decades. It is not a weight-loss diet, though modest weight loss is common; its purpose is to lower blood pressure through the everyday food on the plate. [1]

What the evidence shows

In the original DASH trial, participants who ate the plan for eight weeks lowered systolic blood pressure by an average of 5.5 mmHg — and those with stage 1 hypertension by more than 11 mmHg. Effects were visible within two weeks. [2]

Editor's note: The claim about blood-pressure change in the first two weeks needs a fresher secondary source. Ellen has flagged this for the medical reviewer.

How to build a DASH plate

The plan asks for more of what most Western diets already lack — vegetables, fruit, whole grains, low-fat dairy — and less of what they usually have too much of: salt, red meat, added sugar.

Every day

Aim for 4–5 servings of vegetables, 4–5 servings of fruit, and 6–8 servings of whole grains — not as targets to hit, but as the frame that pushes the sodium and saturated fat down naturally.

A worked example

Breakfast: oat porridge with berries and a spoon of almond butter.
Lunch: chickpea and roasted vegetable bowl with brown rice.
Dinner: grilled salmon, quinoa, and greens dressed with lemon.

Who it isn't for

Anyone on a potassium-restricted diet (typically due to chronic kidney disease) should not start DASH without medical advice — the plan is deliberately potassium-rich. [3]
